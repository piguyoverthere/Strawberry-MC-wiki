Rendering Changes

UI

Crafting Tables

Tabs

To compensate and keep the Crafting Table organized while still allowing it to retain many uses in this mod, the Crafting Table now has static tabs located on a bar.

These tabs group the different things that can be crafted, such as blocks and items. The Forging tab remains locked when a Crafting Table is crafted. It must be unlocked by putting the Crafting Table in another Crafting Table and combining it with 3 Cobblestones in any of the cardinal directions of the crafting grid, a Forging Hammer filling the last cardinal direction, and 4 Metal Ingots [source text truncated].

The Forging tab allows players to forge weapons or equipment from any Metal they have, or combine Metals to form alloys with special properties, using the Crucible tool. The Forging section of the Crafting Table requires a temperature of 1,300 degrees, and all tools require this heat. In Forging mode, the recipe book in Java Edition or the sidebar in Bedrock Edition displays forgeable recipes and alloys in 2 subtabs. The player can select an option and provide a fuel source to create the alloy or equipment. Each recipe takes 15 seconds to forge.

Furnace

The furnace UI for all gamemodes has been changed for 2 output slots, a slot for any slag in realism mode and a slot for the product item. The furnace UI also now allows for the selection of output products, such as choosing to smelt Sand into charred Sand or Glass. The recipe book that tells the player what they can smelt is now turned into a sidebar allowing the player to click a recipe instead of dragging the items into the top slot of the furnace

Flora

Cloudcover

Cloudcover now determines weather, with a new way to render clouds. Clouds are now more 3d, floating in the direction of the wind. Clouds are rendered via block models of pure white that are 32x32 pixels, so 2 blocks wide and 2 blocks tall.

Precipitation

Clouds are always in the sky before any precipitation, at the edge of the air front containing precipitation, are darker and thicker in color, with less light reaching the terrain below. Note overcast weather can still occur independently when an air mass with precipitation dies.

Precipitation is also now locally determined, meaning different superbiomes [see also: Terrain Generation changes] can vary in precipitation.

Wind

Wind now has 3 levels: Breeze Gust Cyclone These 3 levels of wind will trigger different effects in rendering in the game. At wind level 1, grass and other flora as well as leaf falling particles will start to move in the direction of the [see also: wind particles] wind (land breeze or sea breeze). At wind level 2, wind particles will start to dot the lower atmosphere, and c smoke will start to drift in the direction of the wind. Torches may flicker from the strong gust. Rain starts to fall diagonally, which is more dramatic as the wind level increases. At wind level 3, torches become unusable(as they will blow out), and dust particles will form near the ground. If Sand, barren soil, or Dirt is next to a grass block or other vegetation, it will die and turn into a soil block. Standing down wind of snow will give the player frostbite and not wearing wool armor in areas colder than 7 will slowly drain health.

Note that the player takes more hunger moving against the wind, but less hunger than usual moving in the same direction as the wind.

Cauldron With Water

Heating Bubbles

Heating bubbles in cauldrons occur when a cauldron is placed on top of a heat source such as a fire, campfire, magma block, or lava. Items placed inside will become boiled, [see also: stoneshroom] such as hydrated stoneshrooms, boiled eggs, boiled chicken, and more. Water boiling in a cauldron will slowly evaporate, leaving behind less water. A trick the player can use is by putting a dripstone that will slowly refill the cauldron as the water evaporates. Bubble particles occur when the water is heated to full after a heat source is placed (ranges from 1-3 real life minutes).
