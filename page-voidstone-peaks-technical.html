Voidstone Peaks [technical]

Overview

Voidstone Peaks is the biome strawberrymc:voidstone_peaks in The Void. It uses the temperature range -0.1 to 1 and the humidity range -0.15 to 1, with every other climate parameter at 0. Its terrain comes from extra density functions in the void_terrain datapack, generated by tools/void_terrain/gen.py. All of them are scaled by one function, peaks_mask, so the mountains rise only inside the biome.

peaks_mask

The multi_noise biome source picks the biome whose climate point is nearest. peaks_mask rebuilds that comparison in density functions: it measures how much nearer Voidstone Peaks is than the closest other biome, giving 1 inside the biome and fading to 0 at its edges.

ABYSSAL = (-0.5, 0)
ECHOING = (0.4, -0.5)
PEAKS_T, PEAKS_H = (-0.1, 1), (-0.15, 1)
climate = lambda n: {"type": "minecraft:flat_cache", "argument": {"type": "minecraft:shifted_noise", "noise": f"minecraft:{n}", "xz_scale": 0.25, "y_scale": 0, "shift_x": "minecraft:shift_x", "shift_y": 0, "shift_z": "minecraft:shift_z"}}
T, H = climate('temperature'), climate('vegetation')
dist_point = lambda p: add(sq(add(T, -p[0])), sq(add(H, -p[1])))
outside = lambda v, lo: mx(0, add(lo, mul(-1, v)))
dist_peaks = add(sq(outside(T, PEAKS_T[0])), sq(outside(H, PEAKS_H[0])))
fns['peaks_mask'] = {"type": "minecraft:flat_cache", "argument": clamp(mul(10, add(mn(dist_point(ABYSSAL), dist_point(ECHOING)), mul(-1, dist_peaks))), 0, 1)}

As a formula, mask = clamp(10 * (min(d_abyssal, d_echoing) - d_peaks), 0, 1). Each distance is a squared distance in the temperature and humidity plane. Because the Peaks ranges reach past the top of the climate noise, d_peaks only measures how far a value lies below the lower bound of each range. It uses the same temperature and vegetation noise settings as the dimension's noise router, so the mask lines up with biome selection.

Ridges and mountain height

Height is expressed in units of 30 blocks above y=34. Each ridge is 1 - |noise|, which peaks along the zero line of the noise, and squaring it sharpens the crest.

ridge = lambda n: add(1, mul(-1, ab(noise(n, 1, 0))))
fns['peaks_height'] = {"type": "minecraft:flat_cache", "argument": mul(df('peaks_mask'), add(1, add(mul(6, sq(ridge('void_peaks'))), mul(2.5, sq(ridge('void_spires'))))))}
fns['mountains'] = add(add(grad(34, 0, 304, -9), add(df('peaks_height'), -1)),
   mul(df('peaks_mask'), add(mul(0.9, noise('void_spires', 1, 0.2)), mul(0.4, noise('void_crags', 1, 1)))))

With the mask at 1, peaks_height - 1 reduces to the two ridge terms. Their combined maximum of 8.5 units places the highest crest at y=289 before the spire and crag noise is added. Where the ridge terms are small the mountain surface is at y=34, which is below the plain, so the plain's own surface shows through.

Canyons and tunnels

The mountains are combined with two functions by min, turning them into a maze. Only the mountain branch is cut, so the plain underneath stays intact:

fns['canyons'] = mul(14, add(mn(ab(noise('void_canyons_a', 1, 0)), ab(noise('void_canyons_b', 1, 0))), -0.035))
fns['tunnels'] = mul(10, add(mx(ab(noise('void_tunnels_a', 1, 0.7)), ab(noise('void_tunnels_b', 1, 0.7))), -0.07))
fns['fractured_plain'] = mn(mn(mx(df('ground'), mn(df('mountains'), mn(df('canyons'), df('tunnels')))), df('underside')), df('cracks'))

A canyon follows the zero line of either 2D noise, so the two networks cross into a winding pattern that reaches down to the plain. A tunnel requires both 3D noises to be near zero together, which produces thin passages through the rock. The cracks and underside of the plain cut the mountains as well, because they are applied after the mountain branch.

Pillars

The pillars function, described on The Void, adds a mask term:

mul(df('peaks_mask'), add(1.4, mul(0.6, noise('void_crags', 1, 1))))

Outside the biome a pillar needs void_pillars > 0.85. With the mask at 1 and crags at 0 the condition falls to void_pillars > 0.5, so pillars are much more common and thicker, and the 3D crag noise, with weight 0.6, makes their sides rough.
