Realism Mode

Realism Mode is a difficulty option in Strawberry MC. Realism mode encorporates realistic features that make the game harder.

About

The first step for the player to survive in realism mode is to obtain flint tools. In order to obtain flint tools, the player must break gravel to obtain flint chips, which for every 4 flint chips, can be crafted into a flint shard. These flint shards as well as plant fiber[see also: plant fiber] with sticks that can be found by punching leaves are used to craft a flint hatchet.[see also: flint dagger]

Using this hatchet, the player can proceed to use the hatchet to harvest logs (very slow). From these logs and a flint hatchet in the player’s inventory crafting grid, these logs are crafted into wooden planks. Note that the flint hatchet takes 5 durability out of 15 durability to make the wooden planks. These planks along with flint shards from mining and crafting gravel, can be used to craft a basic crafting table (referred to as Workbench). This basic crafting table can be used to directly craft logs into planks.

Assuming the player survives to this far into their journey, the player now must use the dagger to slowly damage and kill sheep. The sheep once killed will turn into a sheep corpse, which can be placed in the crafting table for raw mutton, animal hide, and wool. Along with more planks crafted in the crafting table and fiber, the player can make a basic bed, which allows the player to sleep from midnight to dawn. This basic bed can be upgraded to a comfortable bed that allows the player to sleep before dusk and wake up after dawn.


Once the player passes their first night, they must be cautious about splinters, tripping, and they also need a food source. Depending on the biome, different fruits, berries, vegetables, and grains can be found. Meat and animal products are not recommended as obtaining a furnace can be hard with starter items. For food, look near charred areas for roasted ember lily tubers and burn morels, jungle areas for bananas and grains, taigas and colder biomes for berries, and more[see also: food mechanics]. A water filter is also recommended; water filters can be found in ruins around dry or hot biomes or be crafted later in the game. Once a water filter is obtained, the player can then get charcoal from charred forests and use sand, red sand, gravel, or suspicious mediums to filter water in bottles (obtained from ruins' chests).


When the player can finally support themselves and have time that is not used for gathering food and surviving, the player can finally build a base. Starter bases can be carved into hills and mountains with plentiful soil, or a log cabin made from surrounding logs. At this point, the player can use 2 flint trowels

After halfway through, the stone will drop rocks, and to completely mine the stone, another flint trowel is needed which at this point will drop another rock item. 3 rocks obtained from using the flint trowel can be crafted in a stone cutter from a village for 1 cobblestone slab, or 6 rocks can be placed in the way a trapdoor is crafted in a basic crafting table to result in 1 cobblestone slab.

Once the player obtains a cobblestone slab, the cobblestone slab can be placed at the bottom center slot of the crafting table with one plank of any wood type above; and 3 sticks surrounding the plank like so:


This will result in 1 unlit campfire, which the player can ignite using a flint knife and a sharp rock. Sharp rocks can be obtained by either inserting 2 rocks into a crafting table for 1 sharp rock or by inserting 1 rock into a mason villager’s stone cutter to get 1 sharp rock. But before lighting the campfire, the player needs to find tin ore. Tin ore can be found on cliff faces and can be mined with a flint trowel. Raw tin ore can then be placed on the campfire which can be lit, to obtain pig tin nuggets for every two tin ore. One pig tin nugget can be crafted with1 any fibrous material such as plant fibers or rope along with one stick to obtain a tin hatchet.

A tin hatchet has 15 durability, which must be used to mine iron ore, which now can directly generate under dirt on the surface. A flint trowel can break 4 dirt before breaking, so 4 stone can be revealed to expose possible iron ore since dirt in realism mode is only 1 block thick. If any iron ore is revealed, the tin hatchet can be used to obtain iron ore nodules, which take 2 durability to mine. 3 Iron ore nodules can be combined to create 1 iron ore block. Only iron ore blocks can be smelted in basic furnaces. A basic furnace can be made by lining the base of the crafting grid with cobblestone slabs followed by an upside-down U shape of clay balls above, leaving one empty space in the middle. The clay balls can be obtained by using a tin hatchet or trowel to excavate clay generating under snow on mountainsides and in freshwater rivers as well as sometimes in pockets under or in sand banks.


After the basic furnace is created, the player must leave the furnace to dry for 5 real life minutes.


Coal collected from riverbeds is used as a fuel source, and the iron ore block is inserted into the furnace. The furnace has enough durability for 3 iron blocks to be smelted. Each iron ore block outputs 2 iron ingots and 3-5 slag.

Food Mechanics

In realism mode of Strawberry Minecraft, the player must eat a diet consisting of balanced nutrition to survive. If the player does not eat nutritiously over 3 days, the player has a 70% chance for each day without eating the needed food source to gain the malnourished effect, which can have a wide variety of side effects depending on what the player is deficient in. The good in-game food sources are:

  • Apples
  • Potatoes
  • Melon Slices
  • Bottle of Coconut Milk
  • Coconut (flesh)
  • Raw Mushrooms
  • Sweet Berries
  • Juniper Berries [see also: Juniper]
  • crops
  • Wild Oats[realism exclusive]
  • Lava Seared meats
  • Roasted mushrooms
  • Boiled Eggs[see also: Boiling eggs] [realism exclusive]
